# Dis-tribution (Izdebska Lens)

## Overview

Applies the published analytical frameworks of Viktoria Izdebska (viktoriaizdebska.substack.com and her operating practice dis-tribution.com; two-time founder, AI rollup operator inside a PE advisory firm) to any idea, company, vertical, or space. This is an **attributed idea lens, not voice imitation**: the output is your analysis using her frameworks, cited to her, never writing as her or in her name. Full article texts live in `references/articles/` for direct quotes; `references/corpus-map.md` indexes which article carries which framework.

Her core stance in one line: **product and technology are commoditizing; the only durable asset is an economically validated relationship with a specific customer segment, and owning that distribution is a different problem from keeping it.**

## When NOT to Use

- Drafting content published under her name or mimicking her prose (refuse; that is impersonation).
- Domains her corpus never touches (consumer social, deep tech R&D, crypto). Say the lens is out of its home domain and apply it only where it genuinely transfers.

## The Worldview (axioms behind every analysis)

1. **Distribution is the last moat.** AI lets three people build what took thirty. Acquirers now pay for access to validated user bases, not code or teams.
2. **Signups are not relationships.** Only economically validated relationships count: payment history, retention, switching costs. "Good and for everyone is the same as invisible."
3. **Owning distribution and keeping it are different problems.** Value in service businesses lives in relationships, habits, and people; it can walk out the door post-acquisition (the "organ rejection" question).
4. **Cost advantages are ephemeral, revenue-side synergies are durable.** Competitors implement the same AI; the industry re-prices to the new cost structure.
5. **Diagnosis over ideology.** Strategy questions are spectrum placements, not binaries. Sustainable beats spectacular.
6. **The market always tells you what matters.** Behavioral signals beat firmographics; who is writing checks reveals where value lives.
7. **The tool depreciates; the panel compounds.** Software is rebuilt in weeks and operations are replicated in years, but the behavioral data panel a distribution network generates is time-locked: "history is the one thing money can't compress."
8. **Distribution can be manufactured, on the right terms.** Her thesis here has moved twice (see thesis history in `references/corpus-map.md`); the current version requires a pre-committed buyer, behavioral qualification on usage, and zero-integration-debt transfer. Speculative audience-building without a buyer remains a rejected idea.

## The Run: 10 Diagnostic Passes

Run every pass against the target idea/space, in order. Each pass names its framework and source article. Skip a pass only by stating why it does not apply.

1. **Commoditization check.** What in this space is being commoditized by AI, and what survives? If the product is the claimed moat, ask whether users genuinely cannot get it elsewhere. (The Only Moat Left Is the Customer)
2. **Automation-grade placement.** Estimate the % of the vertical's work AI can actually do. Below 30%: cannot underwrite the transformation. 30 to 70%: rollup/acquisition sweet spot (people plus processes plus meaningful AI leverage). Above 70%: grow organically; acquiring means buying headcount you are about to automate away. The grade, plus PMF status, plus product architecture (custom workflow automation vs off-the-shelf SaaS) determines build vs roll up vs stay away. (Roll Up or Build)
3. **Who is the real customer?** Ask whether the party being monetized is actually distribution that should ride free. Find the third party who benefits in a way nobody else does (data buyers, robotics companies, future acquirers) and price them instead: three parties, one pays, two ride free, distribution builds at maximum velocity. Check who legally owns the monetized asset (data usually belongs to the end client; consent must be designed in). Weigh vertical selection by the multiplier effect: a firm that serves other firms (tax advisors, MSPs, property managers) gives reach *through* the network, so score verticals as fragmentation times end-customer leverage, not fragmentation alone. (The Next Billion Dollar Company; The Third Evolution of Software Pricing)
4. **Know your game.** Is this a global game or an exit game in a small market? If exit: name the likely acquirer now, identify the one thing they will pay for (usually market penetration/distribution, rarely software), and strip investment in everything they will replace. Check whether the well-played small game franchises across parallel markets. (The Next Billion Dollar Company)
5. **Relationship dependency audit.** Split the revenue/user base into firm-loyal, habit-loyal, and person-loyal; estimate the gravity well of key individuals. What survives an ownership or platform transfer? Retention post-transfer ranks: outcome improvement first, seniority/knowledge, options/support, and existing relationship only fourth. (The $3B Blind Spot; Roll Up or Build)
6. **Thrasio stress test.** Score against the five structural failure modes of debt/velocity rollups: cash burn vs transformation speed, diligence quality at deal pace, projections priced on peak conditions (including "AI keeps improving"), growth-at-all-costs vs integration capacity, and operational spread across too many verticals. Speed and integration quality are inversely correlated. (Thrasio Raised $3B)
7. **Durability check.** Is the edge cost-side (ephemeral, competed away) or revenue-side (durable)? Does the customer experience "works better, costs less" without the AI being visible? Two metrics matter: churn on the acquired/served base and free cash flow doubling within about eighteen months. (Why Could AI Rollups Fail; Roll Up or Build)
8. **Buyer and absence map.** Who is actively buying access in this space (AI rollup platforms, PE buy-and-build, vertical SaaS at $5 to 30M ARR inflection), and who is notably absent? The behavioral tell for a distribution buyer: acquired in the last 18 months for the user base and felt integration pain. Absences are evidence, not gaps. When comparing acquisition to alternatives, price distribution at the adjusted cost per *surviving* user (after churn, migration timelines, team attrition, codebase retirement), not the sticker price. (Who's Buying Distribution; dis-tribution.com)
9. **Value-capture level placement.** Place the idea on her three levels. L1, sell the tool: SaaS capturing 2 to 5% of value created, depreciating as AI collapses rebuild cost. L2, sell the outcome: run the function yourself and sell the result (meetings, filed returns, managed properties), capturing 30 to 60% because the customer can no longer comparison-shop; owning the business you operate (Lessin/Metropolis) is L2 with better economics, not a new level. L3, the prediction economy: the behavioral data panel only a scaled distribution network generates. If L3 is in reach, force the pick-one discipline: sell the signal (clean, bounded; benchmark-business exits like Keypoint or RealEstateAPI) or deploy the signal as an origination engine for your own acquisitions (compounding, capital-hungry). Running both poisons the customer base. The stack: L1 spreads the network, L2 funds it and generates the panel, L3 is the appreciating asset. (The Third Evolution of Software Pricing)
10. **Agentic window timing.** Agentic AI workflows are beginning to auto-route users to tools and systems of record; within roughly 18 to 24 months distribution mechanics may look fundamentally different. Ask whether the distribution asset under analysis is being built before or after agents intermediate tool discovery, whether it survives that shift, and whether the window is the opportunity or the kill risk. Distribution assets built pre-intermediation may be structurally different from anything after. (dis-tribution.com)

## Verdict Style (mandatory)

- End with a **placement, not a rating**: where the idea sits on her spectrums and what game it is actually in.
- Include a **"Where this thesis breaks"** section: she revises her own thesis publicly (see thesis history in `references/corpus-map.md`; the current manufactured-distribution version hinges on an unproven conversion assumption she names herself). Every analysis must name the assumptions that, if false, kill the conclusion.
- Include **honest caveats** and what is **notably absent** from the space.
- Cite which article each major claim channels. Where the lens runs beyond her published views, label the extrapolation explicitly.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Generic SWOT/market analysis with her vocabulary sprinkled on | Every section must be driven by one of the 10 passes |
| Citing her V1 "sell manufactured audiences" idea as her position | Use thesis V2 (pre-sold buyer, behavioral qualification); V1 was rejected by buyers and superseded |
| Treating rollup vs organic as a binary | Place it on the automation-grade spectrum with a number |
| Counting signups/users as distribution | Demand payment history, retention, switching costs |
| Scoring the acquisition and ignoring the integration | Pass 5 and 6 are where most ideas die; weight them accordingly |
| Presenting her frameworks as your own or as consensus | Attribute; she is one perspective, and the verdict says so |
